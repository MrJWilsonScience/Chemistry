Sodium thiosulfate and hydrochloric acid

Aim: find out how the concentration of sodium thiosulfate, and the temperature, affect the rate of reaction.

Na₂S₂O₃(aq) + 2HCl(aq) → 2NaCl(aq) + S(s) + SO₂(g) + H₂O(ℓ)
Method and safety
  1. Measure the chosen volume of 0.15 mol l⁻¹ sodium thiosulfate and make it up to 50 cm³ with water.
  2. Warm the beaker in a water bath if you are testing temperature.
  3. Stand the beaker on the paper cross. Add 5 cm³ of 2.0 mol l⁻¹ hydrochloric acid and start the clock.
  4. Look down through the beaker. Stop the clock when you can no longer see the cross.
  5. Record the time. Relative rate = 1/time.

Safety: sulfur dioxide is toxic and irritates the lungs. Use a well-ventilated lab and pour the mixture into the sink with plenty of water, or into a fume cupboard waste container.

Enthalpy of combustion of alcohols

Aim: measure the enthalpy of combustion of the alcohols methanol to pentan-1-ol and compare your values with the data booklet.

Eh = c m ΔT    c = 4.18 kJ kg⁻¹ °C⁻¹    ΔH = −Eh ÷ n
Method and safety
  1. Put the chosen volume of water in the copper can. 1 cm³ of water has a mass of 1 g.
  2. Weigh the spirit burner with its cap on. Note the starting temperature of the water.
  3. Light the burner under the can. Watch the thermometer.
  4. Put the flame out with the cap. Note the highest temperature reached.
  5. Weigh the burner again. Calculate the mass of alcohol burned.

Safety: alcohols are highly flammable. Keep burner caps on when not lit, never refill a lit or hot burner, and wear eye protection.

Vitamin C content by redox titration

Aim: find the mass of vitamin C (ascorbic acid, GFM 176 g) in a tablet or in orange juice by titrating against iodine solution.

C₆H₈O₆ + I₂ → C₆H₆O₆ + 2H⁺ + 2I⁻
Method and safety
  1. Tablets have been dissolved and made up to 250 cm³ in a standard flask. Juice is used directly.
  2. Fill the burette with iodine solution and note the initial reading.
  3. Pipette a sample into the conical flask and add starch indicator.
  4. Add iodine until one drop turns the whole solution permanently blue-black. Note the final reading.
  5. Do a rough titration, then repeat until you have two concordant titres (within 0.20 cm³).

Safety: iodine solution stains skin and clothing and is harmful if swallowed. Use a pipette filler and wear eye protection.
